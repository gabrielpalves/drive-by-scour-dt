Drive-by monitoring infers the condition of a bridge from the dynamic response
of a passing vehicle. Recent studies have classified combinations of scour,
bearing damage, and cracks in railway bridges from on-board vertical
accelerations with convolutional neural networks (CNNs), but the reported
accuracies refer to one tuned network per sensor, two sensor positions, and the
best of several training runs. This paper reconstructs the ten-class benchmark
of Fernandes et al.\ (2025) and extends it to six responses of the leading
vehicle and to a CNN with multi-rate pooling. The dataset comprises 2,000
simulated passages of a five-vehicle train over a 39.9~m two-span bridge,
generated with a train--track--bridge interaction model under varying speed,
temperature, and vehicle properties, with one fixed rail profile.
Hyperparameters are tuned by Bayesian optimization, and every configuration is
trained with 20 random initializations on the same partition of 800 training,
200 validation, and 1,000 test passages. The published accuracies were not
recovered: with the two source channels, the best of 20 runs reached at most
71.8\% for the carbody and 73.8\% for the front bogie, against published
best-run accuracies of up to 86.7\% and 82.2\%. The published finalist
architectures, retrained on the same data with PyTorch or Keras defaults,
reached at most 63.4\% and 67.8\%. The carbody pitch rate, which
the source study did not use, gave the highest mean accuracy in five of the six
combinations of network and tuning strategy. With channel-specific tuning and
multi-rate pooling, it reached a mean accuracy of 77.63\% with a standard
deviation of 2.24 percentage points, no collapsed run, and 43,642 parameters.
Bearing damage and scour level were recognized in 99.97\% and 97.63\% of the
test passages, whereas the crack label limited the accuracy (77.76\%), and the
bogie pitch rates remained near the chance level. Two-channel inputs did not
systematically outperform the better of their two single channels. The results
support reporting complete run distributions and including pitch rates in
drive-by monitoring.
