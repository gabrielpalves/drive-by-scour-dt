Scour --- erosion around bridge foundations during floods --- is a leading
cause of bridge failure but is difficult to inspect. Drive-by monitoring uses
an in-service train's vibration to infer support condition. Previous studies
established feasibility but leave key pre-deployment choices unresolved:
whether to process raw or compressed signals, which neural components help,
and which response channels are informative. This study addresses
those questions with a prospectively specified, source-locked experiment based
on two-dimensional train--track--bridge simulations of two- and four-span
geometries. Four blocks separate scour-only from tasks adding nominal bearing
fixity and a local flexural-rigidity-loss surrogate. One generated
FRA-class-4 profile realization is shared by every semantic state and passage.
Speed, temperature, and vehicle properties vary
between passages, while track damage, wheel polygonization, and suspension
damage are deferred.
A 16-cell factorial compares raw and Piecewise Aggregate Approximation inputs,
positional encoding, recurrence, and fixed-width multi-rate pooling. A sealed,
state-grouped protocol then screens six learning-eligible single channels
descriptively and all 15 eligible two-channel pairs for four retained
pipelines. Two additional constrained-wheelset kinematic proxies remain in the
eight-response payload only as diagnostics and never enter learning or
selection.
